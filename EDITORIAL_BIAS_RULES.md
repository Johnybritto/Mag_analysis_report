# Editorial Bias & Reliability Rules — Mandatory for Every Mag Analysis

This is a mandatory quality-control section for every future magazine analysis.

## Why this exists

The original Mag Analysis framework already required:
- Editorial Framing
- Signal vs Noise
- Hard Metrics & Evidence
- Projections vs Opinions
- separation of ads / low-signal pages
- fact vs inference separation
- caution around promotional or one-sided material

Those checks must now be made explicit as a scored Editorial Bias & Reliability section so they cannot be silently skipped.

## Mandatory section

Every Mag Analysis must include a section titled:

`## Editorial Bias & Reliability — Mandatory`

## Overall Bias Score

Score the full issue from 0–100:

- 0–25: Low bias — balanced, evidence-led, multiple viewpoints, limited promotional framing
- 26–50: Moderate bias — useful evidence but some selective framing, optimism/pessimism, or weak counterarguments
- 51–75: High bias — material one-sidedness, promotional framing, selective evidence, or heavy reliance on projections/management claims
- 76–100: Very High bias — strongly promotional, ideological, advertorial, evidence-light, or materially misleading through omission/framing

Always state both the numeric score and label.

## Mandatory bias checks

Assess at minimum:

1. Source diversity — are multiple independent viewpoints used, or mostly management/promoter/industry voices?
2. Counterarguments — are risks, failures, competing evidence and bear cases presented?
3. Promotional / advertorial tone — does the article read like PR, sponsored content, awards coverage or company promotion?
4. Hard data vs projections — how much of the thesis is supported by audited/current data versus forecasts and aspirations?
5. Selective use of facts — are favourable metrics highlighted while margins, debt, cash flow, receivables, valuation or execution risks are ignored?
6. Political / policy framing — does policy coverage become advocacy instead of evidence-based analysis?
7. Bullish / bearish skew — is the issue systematically pushing optimism or pessimism beyond what evidence supports?
8. Recency and evidence quality — are claims based on current filings/data or stale secondary sources?
9. Missing stakeholder perspective — customers, competitors, regulators, employees, investors, creditors or opposing experts where relevant.
10. Sponsored / low-signal material — do not treat ads, awards, event coverage, generic leadership praise or brand pieces as strong evidence.

## Article-level bias

For high-impact articles, assign an article-level bias label when useful:

- Low
- Moderate
- High
- Very High

Prioritise article-level bias checks for cover stories, investment-relevant sector stories, company profiles/interviews, policy pieces, rankings and articles that generate Phase 3 candidates.

## Required separation

Every material conclusion should distinguish:

- **Magazine claim** — directly stated in the issue
- **Verified fact** — supported by primary/credible evidence where external verification was requested or necessary
- **Analytical inference** — our interpretation of the issue
- **Promotional / editorial framing** — wording or emphasis that may bias the reader
- **External verification needed** — current valuation, price, holdings, order book, financials or other facts not supported by the issue itself

Never convert an editorial claim, management projection or inferred beneficiary into a verified fact.

## Bias impact on investment conclusion

End the section with one explicit judgment:

- `Bias does not materially change the investment conclusion`
- `Bias weakens confidence but does not invalidate the thesis`
- `Bias materially weakens the thesis — external verification required`
- `Bias is too high to use this article as an investment signal`

Explain the reason briefly.

## Integration with existing Mag Analysis rules

This rule does not replace existing sections. It strengthens them.

- `DATA & CLAIMS` still separates hard metrics from projections/opinions.
- `RELEVANCE & FRAMEWORK` still includes Editorial Framing.
- `Signal vs Noise` still ranks evidence quality.
- `Negative Signals / Avoid Traps` still captures adverse evidence.
- `Fact vs Inference Rule` still applies.

The Editorial Bias & Reliability section is the explicit summary judgment that combines these checks into a measurable conclusion.
