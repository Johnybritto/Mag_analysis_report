# Phase 3 — Max Healthcare Institute

Date: 17 August 2026

## Executive conclusion

Max Healthcare is one of the strongest hospital operators in India on operating performance: high bed occupancy, high revenue per occupied bed, strong margins, a large metro footprint, credible acquisition integration and a very large expansion pipeline. The business is high quality.

The stock is a different question. Around the latest reliable late-July 2026 reference price near ₹1,100, Max traded at roughly 70–75 times trailing statutory earnings. That means the market is already paying for several years of strong growth. The new regulatory debate around private-hospital pricing also raises the risk of paying a very high multiple just as pricing freedom comes under greater scrutiny.

**Current stance: Watch for Dip / high-quality 5-year candidate — no chase.**

The next leg of the thesis depends on whether the large wave of new beds can fill quickly without dragging down margins and returns on the money invested.

---

## 1. What are we really buying?

Max is a premium multi-speciality hospital network concentrated heavily in large metro markets. As of May 2026 it had 6,000+ beds across 21 facilities, with roughly 73% of beds in metros.

The core economic model is simple:

1. Build or acquire hospital capacity.
2. Attract strong doctors and specialist teams.
3. Fill beds with complex treatments such as cancer, cardiac, orthopaedics and neurosciences.
4. Increase the amount earned from each occupied bed through higher-value procedures and patient mix.
5. Use strong cash generation from mature hospitals to fund new hospitals and acquisitions.

Max also has smaller adjacent businesses such as Max Lab, Max@Home and a proprietary digital platform, but the hospital network remains the main driver.

---

## 2. Why the Business Today thesis is valid

The Business Today 30 August 2026 issue highlighted Max as one of the strongest operators in its hospital comparison, with roughly 76% occupancy and average revenue per occupied bed around ₹77,800.

The key insight is not merely that Max is adding beds. It already runs a highly utilised premium hospital network and is attempting a major capacity expansion from that strong starting point.

The investment question is therefore:

> Can Max add thousands of beds without destroying occupancy, margins, cash generation or return on the money invested?

---

## 3. Plain-English hospital economics

### Occupancy
Occupancy means how much of the available bed capacity is actually being used by patients.

A 76% occupancy rate means roughly 76 out of every 100 available beds are occupied on average.

For a hospital, very low occupancy wastes expensive buildings, equipment and staff. Very high occupancy can create waiting times and operational pressure. Max currently operates near an attractive level.

### Average revenue per occupied bed
This measures how much revenue Max earns, on average, from one occupied bed for one day. FY26 was roughly ₹78,000.

Higher is usually better only when it comes from a good treatment mix and does not depend on unsustainably aggressive pricing.

### Operating profit margin
Max’s FY26 network operating profit before interest, tax and depreciation was about 26.2% of revenue. In simple language, for every ₹100 of net hospital revenue, roughly ₹26 remained after normal operating costs before interest, tax and depreciation.

### Return on capital employed
Max reports roughly 23% FY26 return on capital on its network calculation, excluding construction-in-progress and with other company-defined adjustments. In plain language, it indicates how efficiently the hospital assets are generating operating returns. This metric must be treated carefully because Max’s presentation includes partner hospitals that are not legally owned by Max.

## 4. FY26 operating performance

### Network numbers disclosed by management

- Gross revenue: ₹10,538 crore, +16% YoY.
- Net revenue: ₹10,065 crore, +16%.
- Operating EBITDA: ₹2,638 crore, +14%.
- Operating margin: 26.2%, versus 26.8% in FY25.
- Network PAT: ₹1,631 crore, +22%.
- Free cash from operations after interest, tax, working-capital changes and routine capex: ₹1,541 crore.
- Net debt: ₹1,908 crore at March 2026; net debt/EBITDA below 1x.

Q4 FY26 occupancy remained above 75%, occupied-bed days grew 8% YoY and average revenue per occupied bed was ₹77,900.

This is strong execution. The slight margin decline is worth tracking because direct costs increased, partly from doctor compensation and staff hired in advance of new capacity.

---

## 5. Important accounting warning — “Network” is not the same as statutory Max

Max publishes a set of “Network” numbers that include Partner Healthcare Facilities. Max has long-term service agreements and significant financial exposure to these facilities, but it does not own or control all of them under accounting rules.

Management believes the Network view better represents operating performance, but these numbers are not the same as the audited statutory consolidated accounts used to value the listed company.

For stock valuation, use statutory consolidated profit and earnings per share. Use Network numbers mainly to understand hospital operations.

This distinction is important because quoting the ₹1,631 crore Network PAT and then calculating a stock P/E on it would overstate the listed company’s statutory earnings.

## 6. Revenue quality

FY26 healthcare-services revenue was roughly ₹9,872 crore. Payor mix was approximately:

- Self-pay patients: 34.2%
- Insurance/corporates: 34.9%
- International patients: 9.1%
- Institutional/government-related: 21.9%

This is reasonably diversified.

International patient revenue is particularly attractive because Max has strong metro locations, specialised care and patient-assistance centres in multiple countries. Q4 international revenue was ₹227 crore, up 12% YoY and about 9% of hospital revenue.

Digital channels also matter operationally: web appointments, digital marketing and lead-management activities were associated with roughly 31% of FY26 revenue. This does not mean 31% is a separate digital business; it means digital channels increasingly acquire and manage patients for the hospital business.

## 7. Specialty mix

FY26 inpatient revenue mix was led by:

- Oncology: 24.1%
- Orthopaedics: 10.9%
- Cardiac sciences: 10.6%
- Neurosciences: 9.6%
- Minimal-access/general surgery: 9.6%

Several specialties grew strongly in FY26: orthopaedics +23%, renal sciences +20%, neurosciences +20%, gastroenterology +29%, cardiac +15% and oncology +9%.

There is one nuance: Max deliberately stopped some high-value chemotherapy drug business for institutional patients after required discounts made the economics unattractive. Management expects oncology’s share to remain around 21–22% rather than return to the prior 25–26%, with other specialties filling the gap.

This is a positive sign of management choosing profit quality over headline revenue.

## 8. Expansion pipeline — the centre of the thesis

Max says it has potential to expand by 8,400+ beds, with roughly 4,000 beds expected over the next 3–4 years. The presentation shows approximately 4,262 bed additions through FY30 and estimated project outflow of about ₹6,672 crore, excluding certain land costs, routine capex and longer-term optional projects.

Indicative additions shown by the company:

- FY27: ~645 beds
- FY28: ~500 beds
- FY29: ~1,458 beds
- FY30: ~1,450 beds

Specific projects include:

- Mohali: ~160-bed brownfield tower, already fully operationalised by May 2026.
- Nanavati Mumbai: 280-bed Phase I tower, with 116 beds operationalised in May and balance expected to follow; another 271-bed phase planned.
- Max Smart Saket: 400-bed brownfield tower, with 156 beds handed to operations by May 2026.
- Lucknow existing hospital: capacity expected to increase from 426 to ~570 beds.
- Gurgaon Sector 56: 500-bed greenfield hospital targeted for commissioning by FY27-end.
- Nagpur: +100 beds targeted FY28.
- Zirakpur/Mohali: ~400 beds targeted FY28.
- Dwarka: +260-bed project.
- Vaishali: +200 beds after approvals.
- Patparganj: +400 beds targeted FY29.
- Lucknow Shaheed Path: board-approved ₹1,400 crore investment for a 700-bed greenfield hospital.
- Pune: proposed ~450-bed super-speciality hospital.

This is a very large growth programme. Execution matters more than announcing capacity.

## 9. Can the new beds actually make money?

Management’s recent experience is encouraging.

Dwarka is the best proof point. The company originally guided toward roughly one year to break even, but it says the facility broke even in about six months. It initially opened around 140 beds out of a 300-bed facility and expanded as occupancy built.

Noida had reached around 64–65% occupancy by Q4 FY26 and management believes it can move toward the network level of around 75%.

Management has explained that new hospitals generally become economically much stronger once occupancy moves beyond the break-even zone because many fixed costs — building, equipment and much of the staff base — do not increase proportionately with each additional patient.

For Gurgaon, Max plans to start with roughly 200 beds rather than opening/staffing all 500 immediately and expects to seek break-even during FY28.

### What we need to watch

The bullish case requires new hospitals to move through this sequence:

50–55% occupancy → break-even zone
60–65% occupancy → economics improving
70–80% occupancy → strong operating leverage

If major new hospitals remain below 50–55% for long periods, the growth programme becomes much less attractive.

## 10. Acquisition execution

Max has a credible acquisition-and-turnaround record. Management says approximately 1,300 beds acquired over the prior 18 months across Lucknow, Nagpur and Noida had been integrated into the network.

Recent commentary indicates:

- Lucknow EBITDA has increased substantially from the level at acquisition.
- Noida has shown meaningful improvement in occupancy and profit.
- Dwarka, while not an acquisition, demonstrates strong greenfield ramp execution.
- Bhubaneswar/Kalinga adds a 250-bed profitable hospital and creates a platform in East India; management said it was generating roughly ₹10 crore annual EBITDA at acquisition and expects meaningful scope to improve it.

Management’s stated acquisition hurdle is roughly 20–25% return on capital within four years.

That is the correct discipline; future Phase 3 reviews should test whether actual acquired hospitals meet it.

---

## 11. Cash flow, debt and capital intensity

The balance sheet is not currently the main concern.

FY26 network free cash from operations was ₹1,541 crore. During the year the company deployed roughly ₹1,627 crore into capacity projects and upgrades, plus land purchases and dividend payments.

At March 2026:

- Gross network debt: ~₹2,924 crore
- Cash/bank: ~₹1,122 crore
- Net debt: ~₹1,908 crore
- Net debt / operating profit: below 1x

Max therefore has borrowing capacity.

However, hospital growth is capital intensive. The planned ₹6,672 crore project outflow means debt and construction spending can rise materially before all new hospitals contribute full profits.

The thesis requires cash generation from mature hospitals to keep funding growth without pushing leverage to uncomfortable levels.

---

## 12. Receivables

Network trade receivables were around ₹1,155 crore at March 2026. Management reported DSO of 87 days, improved from 92 days in September 2025.

In plain language: after Max bills a payer, it takes around 87 days on average to collect the money represented by receivables.

This is worth watching because insurance companies, corporates and government schemes can stretch collection cycles.

Improving toward 70–80 days would be positive. A reversal toward 100+ days while institutional/insurance mix rises would be a warning.

---

## 13. Doctor costs — an underrated risk

Direct costs increased to roughly 41% of FY26 net revenue from 39.4% in FY25. One driver was higher doctor and staffing costs as Max hired ahead of new-bed openings.

Senior doctors are one of the scarcest inputs in premium healthcare. When multiple hospital chains expand simultaneously, they compete for the same clinicians.

Management argues that doctor-cost pressure normally settles as hospitals ramp and revenue catches up. That is plausible, but it needs to show up in the numbers.

If doctor costs keep rising faster than revenue, the capacity boom can become a margin problem even when beds fill.

---

## 14. New regulatory risk — important

In August 2026, Reuters reported that a parliamentary standing committee had recommended benchmarking private-hospital room charges and using fixed package rates for standard medical and surgical procedures. Separately, health-insurance reforms being discussed include more standardised treatment rates and billing transparency.

These are recommendations/discussions, not enacted nationwide price caps as of this review.

Nevertheless, the risk matters more for Max than for a low-priced hospital operator because Max earns one of the highest revenues per occupied bed in the sector.

A high-quality business trading at a premium valuation is especially sensitive to any change that reduces pricing freedom.

This is the biggest new risk that was not visible in the FY26 results.

## 15. Valuation

Latest reliable late-July references place the stock around ₹1,080–₹1,115, with a 52-week range roughly ₹903–₹1,302. Third-party market databases showed approximately 67–75x trailing statutory earnings depending on exact date/data adjustment; Economic Times showed FY26/TTM EPS near ₹14.82 and P/E around 73x at ₹1,084.

Reuters also cited Max among hospital peers trading in roughly the 66–75x FY26 earnings range during the Manipal IPO comparison.

This is expensive.

### Simple FY27 scenario analysis — not management guidance

Using approximate FY26 statutory PAT of ₹1,442 crore and about 97.3 crore shares:

| FY27 statutory PAT scenario | Growth | Approx EPS | P/E at ₹1,100 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ₹1,658cr | +15% | ₹17.0 | ~64.5x |
| ₹1,730cr | +20% | ₹17.8 | ~61.9x |
| ₹1,803cr | +25% | ₹18.5 | ~59.4x |

Even if statutory profit grows 25%, ₹1,100 still implies close to 60x earnings.

That does not mean the stock cannot rise. It means the investor is paying today for a lot of future success.

## 16. Valuation / entry framework

These are research zones, not guaranteed technical supports or price targets.

### Around ₹1,050–1,125
Good company, poor margin of safety. **No chase.**

### Around ₹950–1,000
First serious revalidation zone. At this level I would re-check upcoming results, regulatory news and bed-ramp execution.

### Around ₹850–925
Much more interesting for a 5-year position if the business thesis remains intact. This roughly approaches/breaches the previous 52-week low zone and creates more protection against valuation compression.

### Below ₹850
Do not automatically buy. First determine why it fell. If earnings/occupancy remain healthy and the fall is only market/valuation driven, it could become highly attractive. If pricing regulation or hospital economics have structurally changed, the lower price may be justified.

A higher entry can also become rational if earnings accelerate enough that forward valuation falls materially without a price correction.

## 17. Bull / Base / Bear

### Bull case

- New brownfield beds fill rapidly.
- Gurgaon breaks even quickly like Dwarka.
- Occupancy stays ~75% despite large capacity additions.
- Revenue per occupied bed remains healthy.
- Operating margin stays around 26–27%.
- International patient mix increases.
- Lucknow, Noida, Bhubaneswar and other acquired hospitals approach mature Max economics.
- Cash generation funds the expansion without excessive debt.
- Regulation improves transparency but does not materially cap premium hospital economics.

Result: Max can compound profit at high-teens/20%+ for several years and justify a substantial premium.

### Base case

- Revenue and profit grow 14–18%.
- New beds take longer to ramp.
- Doctor costs remain elevated.
- Operating margin settles around 24–26%.
- Debt rises temporarily with expansion.
- Valuation gradually compresses as the business matures.

Result: business remains excellent, but shareholder returns from a 70x entry may be only moderate because earnings growth is offset by a lower valuation multiple.

### Bear case

- New capacity opens faster than demand/doctor recruitment.
- Network occupancy falls below ~70% for an extended period.
- Operating margin falls below ~23–24%.
- Private-hospital price regulation limits pricing.
- Acquisitions/greenfields absorb cash faster than expected.
- Debt rises while free cash flow weakens.

Result: the market can sharply reduce the valuation multiple even if absolute revenue continues growing.

## 20. What would disprove the thesis?

The single most useful operating warning would be:

> **Network occupancy falling below roughly 70% for several quarters while operating margin falls below about 24% as new capacity comes online.**

That would suggest Max is expanding faster than it can profitably fill beds.

A second major thesis break would be binding regulation that materially reduces Max’s pricing power across a large portion of revenue.

---

## 21. Max vs Poly Medicure

They are different ways to play the same healthcare expansion.

### Max
- Direct hospital operator.
- Strong brand and operating execution.
- Benefits directly from more patients, insurance and medical tourism.
- Very capital intensive.
- Exposed to doctor costs and hospital-price regulation.
- Current valuation very high.

### Poly Medicure
- Supplies recurring medical products/consumables.
- Sells across many hospitals rather than depending on one network.
- Less exposed to hospital price regulation.
- Acquisition integration and working capital are its current issues.
- Also expensive, but the business has a different risk profile.

At present, neither deserves chasing. PolyMed has cleaner second-order economics; Max has stronger demonstrated hospital operating quality.

## Final Phase-3 verdict

**Max Healthcare is a high-quality company and a credible 5-year compounder candidate, but current valuation leaves too little room for mistakes.**

The business is not the concern. The concern is paying 60–75x earnings while Max is entering its heaviest expansion phase and private-hospital pricing is receiving regulatory scrutiny.

### Action

**WATCH FOR DIP — HIGH PRIORITY.**

I would revalidate seriously around ₹950–1,000 and become much more interested around ₹850–925 if occupancy, margins and regulation remain favourable.

Do not buy merely because the stock falls. The best setup is:

> **lower valuation + occupancy still near 75% + operating margin still ~25–27% + new beds ramping + cash generation staying strong.**
