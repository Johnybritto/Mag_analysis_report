# Phase 3 — India Space Basket: Avantel vs MTAR vs Centum vs MIDHANI

Date: 17 August 2026

## Executive Conclusion

These four companies represent four very different ways to monetise India's expanding space ecosystem:

- **Avantel** — potentially the best recurring *service* economics through Ground Segment as a Service (GSaaS), but commercial GSaaS revenue is not yet proven at meaningful scale and the valuation remains demanding.
- **MTAR Technologies** — the strongest operating momentum and critical launch/precision hardware capability, but the current earnings engine is overwhelmingly clean energy/nuclear rather than space. It should not be bought merely because it makes rocket-engine hardware.
- **Centum Electronics** — the best balance of repeat high-reliability electronics, meaningful Defence/Space/Aerospace exposure, strong order-book growth and still-underappreciated space capability; however, FY26 foreign-subsidiary cleanup/discontinued-operation losses must be proven to be behind it.
- **MIDHANI** — the cleanest disclosed proof of actual space revenue among the four and an attractive recurring picks-and-shovels model in titanium/superalloys/fasteners, but growth is slower and valuation already discounts a meaningful strategic-material premium.

### Current research ranking

| Rank | Company | What We Are Really Buying | Space Monetisation Quality | Main Problem | Current Stance |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Centum Electronics** | Repeat high-reliability electronics + strategic systems | High | Canada/Europe cleanup, working capital, pure space revenue undisclosed | **Deep Dive / Watch for confirmation** |
| 2 | **MIDHANI** | Strategic materials + aerospace/space fasteners | High and proven | Valuation vs moderate growth; PSU execution/raw materials | **Watch for Dip** |
| 3 | **Avantel** | SATCOM today + potential GSaaS recurring infrastructure | Potentially very high | GSaaS monetisation unproven; FY26 earnings collapse; expensive | **Speculative Watch — no chase** |
| 4 | **MTAR Technologies** | Precision engineering led by clean energy/nuclear, with critical space hardware | Structurally high but financially small today | Very high valuation/crowding; space is not the current earnings driver | **Excellent business, weak space-entry thesis at current valuation** |

This ranking is specifically for the **space investment thesis**, not a ranking of overall company quality. MTAR currently has the strongest overall operating momentum.

# 1. Avantel Limited

## 1.1 What the business actually is

Avantel is a strategic communications and defence-electronics company with capabilities in satellite communications, RF systems, embedded systems, software-defined radios, radar subsystems, network-management software and after-market support.

The company is moving into a more direct commercial-space infrastructure role through an IN-SPACe-authorised **Ground Segment as a Service (GSaaS)** network. Its stated plan covers fixed and transportable ground stations across VHF, UHF, S, C, X and Ka bands for TT&C, payload-data reception and ranging.

### Evidence classification
- **Hard fact:** Avantel has IN-SPACe authorisation to establish and operate multiple ground stations and is setting up GSaaS infrastructure.
- **Hard fact:** Avantel already has decades of SATCOM/defence communications capability and an indigenous MSS network for the Indian Navy.
- **Inference:** GSaaS can become a recurring infrastructure model if multiple domestic/global satellite operators become paying users.
- **Not yet proven:** material recurring GSaaS revenue or profitability.

## 1.2 Why the recurrence model is attractive

If commercialised successfully, Avantel has the best theoretical recurrence model of the four companies:

Satellite Operator A → repeated ground-station passes

Satellite Operator B → repeated ground-station passes

Constellation grows → more satellites → more passes → more data/TT&C activity

This can potentially create:

**hardware + network deployment + GSaaS fees + AMC/after-market support**

That is economically better than a one-off mission component.

Avantel also won a ₹459.9 crore rate contract from Zetwerk for supply and AMC of Real-Time Train Information System devices for CRIS/Indian Railways. RTIS was developed in collaboration with ISRO and uses satellite technology. This is **not pure space-sector revenue**, but it demonstrates that Avantel can monetise satellite-linked technology with an AMC layer.

## 1.3 Financial reality

FY26 was poor:

- Revenue: about **₹222.9 crore**, down ~10.5% YoY
- PAT: about **₹15.0 crore**, down ~73.4% YoY
- Operating margin: roughly **21.5%**, versus a much higher FY25 base
- Depreciation and other operating costs rose materially as the company expanded capacity.

Q1 FY27 showed a recovery in secondary/market summaries of the exchange-filed result:

- Revenue: roughly **₹70.4 crore**, +~36% YoY
- PAT: roughly **₹5.4 crore**, +~67% YoY

One quarter is not sufficient to establish a durable earnings recovery.

## 1.4 Order / customer quality

The company has substantial strategic-sector relationships and a meaningful new Railway RTIS rate contract with AMC. SATCOM and defence communications provide the existing revenue engine while GSaaS/small satellites provide optionality.

The key mistake would be to count the entire RTIS contract as "space revenue." It is a railway technology contract using satellite infrastructure.

## 1.5 Capacity / capex

Avantel has expanded its physical footprint, including a Hyderabad facility intended to support newer areas such as radar technologies and **small-satellite manufacturing**. The company also maintains clean-room, SMT, environmental test, RF test, CNC and system-integration infrastructure.

The risk is that new capacity creates depreciation and fixed costs before utilisation catches up — exactly what FY26 earnings make us sensitive to.

## 1.6 Management / governance watch

A promoter, Abburi Vidyasagar, sold 500,000 shares in July 2026, reducing the disclosed holding from 14.67% to 14.49%. The sale by itself is not a thesis-breaker, but it belongs in the monitoring file because the stock carries a substantial growth premium.

## 1.7 Valuation / crowding

A late-July market snapshot around ₹160 implied a market capitalisation in the rough ₹4,000–4,300 crore region. Against FY26 PAT of only ~₹15 crore, the historic multiple is extremely high. Even allowing for FY27 recovery, the market is already pricing substantial future growth.

### Valuation conclusion
**Avantel is hidden from a business-model perspective, not from a valuation perspective.**

## 1.8 Bull / Base / Bear

### Bull
GSaaS becomes a genuine commercial network with multiple domestic and foreign constellations; SATCOM/defence orders remain healthy; new facilities gain utilisation; recurring service/AMC revenue rises and PAT normalises strongly.

### Base
Defence/SATCOM and RTIS drive growth, while GSaaS remains a small optional vertical for several years. Earnings recover, but the current valuation absorbs much of the upside.

### Bear
GSaaS remains mostly infrastructure/marketing rather than meaningful revenue; new capacity depresses returns; FY26 was not merely a temporary trough; valuation compresses sharply.

## 1.9 Mandatory KPIs

1. Revenue explicitly attributable to GSaaS / commercial space
2. Number of paying satellite operators / constellations
3. Ground-station utilisation or satellite-pass volume
4. Recurring service / AMC share of revenue
5. Consolidated PAT margin recovery
6. Operating cash flow vs PAT
7. Return on new facilities

### Single disprove metric
**GSaaS remains financially immaterial while valuation continues to assume high growth.**

### Verdict
**SPECULATIVE WATCH — NO CHASE.**

Avantel is the most interesting revenue-model optionality, but today we are paying heavily for a business model that is not yet financially proven.

# 2. MTAR Technologies

## 2.1 What the business actually is

MTAR is a high-precision engineering manufacturer spanning clean energy, civilian nuclear, space, aerospace/defence and other critical-engineering applications.

Its space capability is unquestionably real. The company manufactures:

- Vikas liquid propulsion engine assemblies
- cryogenic engine assemblies
- turbo pumps
- booster pumps
- gas generators
- injector heads
- fluid-control components/modules
- electro-pneumatic modules
- ball screws and hydraulic actuators
- satellite valves and other critical assemblies.

MTAR has supplied ISRO since the 1980s and delivered its first Vikas engine in 1989.

## 2.2 Revenue recurrence

The recurrence mechanism is excellent structurally:

Launch 1 → propulsion/precision hardware

Launch 2 → another set of hardware

Launch 3 → another set

Therefore higher PSLV/GSLV/SSLV/private-launch cadence can generate serial demand.

MTAR is also developing a load-bearing Z-adapter for Thales Alenia Space, with the company indicating that successful first articles could move into volume production under a long-term arrangement.

## 2.3 The crucial correction: space is NOT the current earnings engine

This is the most important Phase 3 finding.

FY26 financials were excellent overall:

- Revenue: **₹876.2 crore**, +29.6%
- EBITDA: **₹171.2 crore**, margin ~19.5%
- PAT: **₹94.0 crore**, +76.2%
- Operating cash flow: roughly **₹196.9 crore**
- FY26 closing order book: about **₹2,581.9 crore**, nearly 3x FY26 revenue.

But FY26 **Aerospace & Defence revenue was only about ₹23.6 crore**, roughly 2.7% of total revenue. The company does not separately disclose pure space revenue in that number.

At 31 March 2026, order-book mix was approximately:

- Clean Energy — fuel cell/hydel/others: 51.2%
- Clean Energy — civil nuclear: 26.3%
- Aerospace & Defence: 14.0%
- Products & others: 8.5%

### Conclusion
The stock is currently primarily an **energy/nuclear precision-engineering growth story with space optionality**, not a space pure-play.

## 2.4 Current business momentum

Q1 FY27 secondary summaries of the company's result/presentation indicate a very strong start, including revenue around ₹361 crore, PAT around ₹50 crore and a sharply higher order book after large inflows. These numbers should be rechecked against the final company presentation before execution of any investment decision, but the direction is clearly strong.

The business quality today is substantially stronger than Avantel's.

## 2.5 Cash flow / debt

FY26 operating cash generation was strong. Debt/equity remains manageable although borrowings have risen as capacity and growth investments increase.

This is a much healthier cash-generation profile than many thematic small/mid-cap manufacturers.

## 2.6 Valuation / crowding

The stock rerated enormously during 2026, at one stage trading above ₹8,000 before correcting sharply. Late-July snapshots around ₹5,200–5,500 still implied a market capitalisation around ₹16,000 crore and a very high historic earnings multiple.

Even with strong Q1 FY27 earnings, the valuation demands continued execution.

### Valuation conclusion
**Great operating company; poor candidate to buy merely because of the space narrative.**

## 2.7 Bull / Base / Bear

### Bull
Clean energy/nuclear scale rapidly, aerospace/defence doubles repeatedly from a small base, Thales/other exports move into serial production, and launch cadence creates growing space hardware revenue.

### Base
Core energy/nuclear execution stays strong but space remains <10% of revenue for years. The company grows well but valuation determines stock returns.

### Bear
Major customer/order concentration or clean-energy demand slows, while investors continue valuing MTAR as a broad strategic-manufacturing hyper-growth story. Multiple compression overwhelms earnings growth.

## 2.8 Mandatory KPIs

1. Aerospace & Defence revenue as % of total
2. Separately disclosed space revenue, if management begins reporting it
3. ISRO/NSIL order intake and launch-vehicle hardware volumes
4. Thales Z-adapter progression into serial production
5. Total order book / revenue
6. Operating cash flow / PAT
7. Customer concentration
8. EBITDA margin through rapid scale-up

### Single disprove metric for the space thesis
**Aerospace/space remains financially tiny despite India's rising launch cadence.**

### Verdict
**EXCELLENT COMPANY — DO NOT BUY IT AS A SPACE STOCK AT A HUGE PREMIUM.**

For us, MTAR becomes attractive on valuation correction or when space/aerospace becomes a visibly material part of earnings rather than a technically impressive but small vertical.

# 3. Centum Electronics

## 3.1 What the business actually is

Centum designs and manufactures high-reliability electronics across Defence, Space, Aerospace, industrial and other high-complexity markets.

Its space credentials are deep rather than promotional:

- products onboard satellites since 2002
- satellite-bus modules for controls, sensors, telemetry, remote control, data handling and power
- payload electronics
- launch-vehicle electronics
- qualification/manufacturing test systems
- microelectronics manufacturing
- power converters and other high-reliability subsystems
- more than 200 mission-critical modules supplied for Chandrayaan-3
- historical missions containing hundreds of Centum modules/subsystems.

This creates genuine **repeat subsystem manufacturing** exposure.

## 3.2 FY26 operating performance

Standalone FY26:

- Operational revenue: **₹973.1 crore**, +25.4%
- Adjusted EBITDA: **₹120.9 crore**
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: **12.4%**
- PBT before exceptional items: **₹100.4 crore**, +62.7%
- Order book: **₹1,644.8 crore**, +22.7%
- ROCE: ~21.2%
- Net working-capital days: ~142.

Industry mix on the standalone business showed **Defence + Space + Aerospace combined at about 50% of revenue**. This is important but must not be misrepresented as 50% pure space revenue.

Centum's BTS/strategic electronics business grew faster than the overall company and its order book continued expanding.

The company also highlighted a second complete radar-system order for satellite and space-debris tracking — useful evidence that its space exposure extends beyond historical ISRO mission association.

## 3.3 The giant FY26 accounting/foreign-subsidiary issue

The apparent consolidated loss needs to be understood correctly.

FY26 consolidated continuing operations were strong:

- Revenue: ~**₹952.7 crore**
- EBITDA: ~**₹135.4 crore**, margin ~14.2%
- PAT from continuing operations: ~**₹100.7 crore**, nearly double YoY.

But discontinued Canada operations generated a large loss, and standalone accounts also carried exceptional write-offs/impairments linked to the foreign operations. Canada was moved toward wind-up and the European business entered judicial restructuring/reorganisation.

### This is the central risk
If the cleanup really ends, Centum can emerge with a far cleaner Indian strategic-electronics business. If more losses/cash leakage continue, the apparent valuation on normalized earnings is misleading.

## 3.4 Revenue recurrence

Centum's recurrence is not subscription-like, but the structure is good:

Satellite A → electronics modules

Satellite B → another set

Launch vehicle → another set

Radar/space-surveillance system → systems + follow-on support

Its 20+ year mission history supports repeat qualification rather than one-off association.

## 3.5 Order-book quality

An order book of ~₹1,645 crore is about **1.7x FY26 standalone revenue**, providing reasonable visibility.

The strategic-electronics/BTS business is the part we care about most for this thesis. The company also has a large HAL AESA radar programme and global high-complexity EMS exposure; these diversify earnings but mean not all order-book growth is space driven.

## 3.6 Working capital / balance-sheet watch

Working capital is heavy:

- inventory and receivables are substantial
- NWC was around 142 days
- standalone borrowings were roughly ₹120 crore current plus a small non-current amount at FY26 year-end.

This must improve as the company scales. High-reliability electronics can look excellent on P&L while absorbing cash in inventory/WIP and receivables.

## 3.7 Valuation / crowding

Late-July snapshots around ₹3,450–3,700 implied a market capitalisation around ₹5,000–5,500 crore.

Using ~₹100.7 crore of FY26 continuing-operation PAT as a rough normalization, the market was valuing the business at roughly **50x normalized continuing earnings**. Reported trailing P/E is distorted by discontinued/exceptional losses.

That is not cheap, but materially less extreme than Avantel or MTAR if the cleanup is truly complete and earnings keep compounding.

## 3.8 Bull / Base / Bear

### Bull
Canada/Europe losses disappear, India strategic electronics grows 20%+, order book expands, space/radar/satellite programmes scale and working capital improves. Normalised earnings become the real reported earnings and valuation becomes much easier to justify.

### Base
Core India business grows well but working capital stays high; space remains bundled with defence/aerospace and foreign cleanup takes longer. Stock compounds mainly with earnings, not rerating.

### Bear
Further foreign-subsidiary losses/write-offs occur, cash remains trapped in working capital and the market had prematurely valued the company on normalized profit.

## 3.9 Mandatory KPIs

1. Any further Canada/Europe cash loss / exceptional charge
2. Continuing-operations PAT versus reported PAT
3. Strategic Electronics / BTS revenue and order-book growth
4. Space-specific orders and revenue disclosure
5. Defence + Space + Aerospace mix
6. Order book / revenue
7. Net working-capital days — target direction below 140 and ideally toward 120
8. Operating cash flow / PAT
9. Borrowings

### Single disprove metric
**Foreign cleanup continues consuming cash/earnings while working capital remains structurally excessive.**

### Verdict
**BEST BALANCE OF THE FOUR FOR FURTHER RESEARCH — BUT WAIT FOR CLEAN FINANCIAL CONFIRMATION.**

Centum is currently the name I would investigate most seriously because the space exposure is technically real, repeatable and embedded inside a broader profitable strategic-electronics platform rather than dependent on one future project.

# 4. MIDHANI

## 4.1 What the business actually is

Mishra Dhatu Nigam (MIDHANI) manufactures critical high-performance materials including:

- titanium and titanium alloys
- nickel-based superalloys
- special steels
- high-strength materials
- forgings/castings
- aerospace-grade fasteners.

This is a classic **picks-and-shovels** position upstream of rockets, satellites, missiles, aero-engines and other strategic systems.

## 4.2 The strongest pure-space revenue evidence of the four

Management disclosed FY26 turnover by end market:

- **Space: ~₹93 crore**
- Defence: ~₹301 crore
- PSUs: ~₹603 crore
- Energy: ~₹2 crore
- Others: ~₹129 crore
- Exports: ~₹78 crore.

Total FY26 turnover was about **₹1,208.6 crore**, so disclosed space revenue was roughly **7.7% of total turnover**.

This is materially better evidence than simply saying a company participated in Chandrayaan.

## 4.3 FY26 performance / order book

- FY26 turnover: ~**₹1,208.6 crore**, +12.5%
- FY26 PAT: **₹130.8 crore**, +18.8%
- Opening FY27 order book: ~**₹2,290 crore**
- Management cited an open order book around ₹2,249–2,250 crore during the June call
- Management targeted at least ~15% FY27 top-line growth, with higher aspiration if raw-material/energy conditions cooperate
- Defence represented about **79% of open order book**, creating concentration but also strong visibility from strategic programmes.

## 4.4 Titanium / strategic-material recurrence

FY26 titanium production reached around **700 tonnes**, roughly double earlier levels, and management disclosed more than **₹660 crore of titanium orders**.

Every new rocket/satellite/missile/aero-engine programme consumes material again. Unlike a ground asset that can operate for years without replacement, strategic alloys become part of the hardware and therefore repeat with serial production.

## 4.5 Fasteners — small today, high-quality recurrence

MIDHANI commissioned a roughly **₹40 crore aerospace-grade fastener facility** aimed particularly at missile and space applications.

Management said:

- orders are already in the pipeline/execution
- expected order book from this sector is roughly **₹20–30 crore per year**
- it sees at least **₹25 crore as a stable annual revenue** level
- MIDHANI has an advantage because it can manufacture its own alloy wire/material rather than importing input wire.

This is exactly the type of recurring revenue we want — small today, but directly connected to repeated serial production.

## 4.6 Moat

MIDHANI's moat is metallurgy, qualification and strategic-material capability, not merely machining.

Management noted that many precision machine shops can machine the final parts, but creating the certified underlying high-performance material is where technical rejection/capability risk lies.

NADCAP heat-treatment certification also improves its ability to sell directly into global aerospace OEM supply chains.

## 4.7 Biggest risks

### Raw-material imports
MIDHANI depends on imported nickel, cobalt, molybdenum, tungsten, chromium, vanadium, rhenium and some titanium sponge. Geopolitical disruptions and price spikes can delay production and hurt fixed-price contracts.

The planned/perpetual "metal bank" is intended to reduce this risk, but cannot eliminate raw-material economics.

### PSU execution / working capital
Order conversion can be slow and receivables/inventory can remain elevated.

### Capex
Management discussed roughly **₹1,000 crore of capex over the coming three years**, focused on modernisation/downstream equipment. This could improve productivity but creates execution and funding risk.

## 4.8 Valuation / crowding

A mid/late-July snapshot around ₹400–410 implied market capitalisation around ₹7,500–7,700 crore and a trailing P/E near the high-50s.

For a company currently guiding roughly mid-teens revenue growth rather than 40–50% growth, this is **not cheap**.

The stock deserves a strategic-material premium, but buying at any price would defeat our hidden-theme discipline.

## 4.9 Bull / Base / Bear

### Bull
Titanium volumes keep scaling, missile/space/aero-engine serial programmes accelerate, fasteners grow from ₹25 crore into a larger value-added vertical, global OEM qualifications convert into exports and new downstream equipment lifts margins/productivity.

### Base
Revenue/PAT compound in the mid-teens; space stays around high-single-digit mix; strategic orders remain healthy but working capital and PSU execution prevent a large rerating.

### Bear
Raw-material/energy disruptions delay execution, fixed-price contracts compress margins, capex absorbs cash and valuation de-rates toward a more typical PSU/industrial multiple.

## 4.10 Mandatory KPIs

1. Space revenue — start from **₹93 crore FY26** and monitor growth
2. Space revenue as % of total
3. Titanium production/dispatch and titanium order book
4. Fastener revenue / annual order book
5. Open order book and conversion
6. Defence concentration
7. Export revenue and global OEM qualifications
8. EBITDA margin
9. Receivable/inventory days
10. Capex and incremental ROCE

### Single disprove metric
**Space/titanium/fastener revenue fails to outgrow the company despite a rising Indian launch/aerospace cadence.**

### Verdict
**WATCH FOR DIP — BEST PROVEN RECURRING SPACE PICKS-AND-SHOVELS EXPOSURE OF THE FOUR.**

We do not need to guess whether space revenue exists: management has disclosed it. The question is whether it grows fast enough to justify a premium valuation.

# 6. Which One Would We Actually Prioritise?

## Best **overall space investment candidate for deeper work**: Centum

Why:
- repeat electronics across satellites/launch vehicles
- long mission history
- significant combined strategic end-market exposure
- solid order book
- continuing operations profitable and growing
- less valuation extremity than MTAR/Avantel.

But do not buy until foreign cleanup and cash conversion are revalidated.

## Best **proven recurring space picks-and-shovels**: MIDHANI

Why:
- disclosed ₹93 crore FY26 space revenue
- materials consumed with each new programme
- titanium scaling
- new space/missile fastener vertical
- stable/repeat order potential.

Problem: valuation is already rich relative to current growth.

## Best **future recurring-service model**: Avantel

Why:
- GSaaS could create repeated revenue from operators/constellations rather than one-off mission hardware.

Problem: this is still optionality, not proven material earnings.

## Best **overall operating business**: MTAR

Why:
- excellent FY26 growth, cash flow and order visibility.

Problem for this specific thesis: the market is paying for the whole strategic-manufacturing growth story, while space is currently a small financial contributor.

# 7. Entry / Tracking Framework

Do **not** buy any of these solely because India's space programme is expanding.

### Centum — upgrade trigger
- no further major foreign-subsidiary cash losses
- continuing-operation growth converts into reported PAT
- NWC days improve
- new space/satellite/radar orders remain visible.

### MIDHANI — upgrade trigger
- valuation correction **or** space/titanium/fastener earnings accelerate materially
- FY27 space revenue clearly above the ₹93 crore FY26 base
- fastener line establishes repeat orders.

### Avantel — upgrade trigger
- first meaningful disclosed GSaaS revenue/customer base
- 2–3 consecutive quarters of PAT recovery
- operating cash flow supports growth
- valuation compresses or earnings catch up.

### MTAR — upgrade trigger for the space thesis
- A&D/space revenue becomes a materially larger part of sales/order intake
- export space programmes move into serial production
- valuation becomes materially more forgiving.

# 9. Data / Evidence Caveats

- Pure space revenue is not separately disclosed by Avantel, MTAR or Centum. Do not invent a percentage from broad Defence/Aerospace/Communications segments.
- MIDHANI management explicitly disclosed FY26 space turnover of ~₹93 crore.
- Avantel GSaaS economics are an analytical inference from the authorised business model; material commercial revenue has not yet been demonstrated in public disclosures reviewed here.
- MTAR's technical space importance is far greater than space's current contribution to consolidated revenue.
- Centum's 50% Defence/Space/Aerospace mix is a combined category, not 50% space.
- Market-price/valuation references are approximate late-July 2026 snapshots, not live execution prices. Refresh price, market cap, trailing/forward earnings and technical levels before any capital deployment.
