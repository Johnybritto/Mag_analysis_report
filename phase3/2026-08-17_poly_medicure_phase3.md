# Phase 3 — Poly Medicure

Date: 17 August 2026

## Executive conclusion

Poly Medicure passes the **business-quality** test much more convincingly than the **valuation / margin-of-safety** test.

The company has a strong recurring medical-device model, meaningful export scale, high operating margins, a robust balance sheet, a broadening product mix and credible adjacency into cardiology and orthopaedics. The key current issue is that acquisitions and international subsidiaries are causing consolidated margins/profits to lag the strong standalone business, while working-capital intensity has worsened and ROCE has fallen from earlier levels.

**Current stance: Watch for Dip / Deep Dive continuation — do not chase.**

At the latest reliable August 2026 market snapshot used for this review, the stock was around ₹1,770 and roughly 50–56x trailing consolidated earnings depending on exact date/source. Even if FY27 execution improves, the valuation still requires sustained high-teens/20%+ profit growth and successful integration of the new acquisitions.

---

## 1. What are we really buying?

Poly Medicure is not simply a hospital-capex supplier. Its core business consists of medical consumables and devices used repeatedly across infusion therapy, dialysis/renal care, blood collection/management, critical care, oncology, vascular access, cardiology, anaesthesia/respiratory, urology, surgery/wound drainage, diagnostics and related therapies.

The investment attraction is **repeat usage**: hospitals buy many products again for each procedure/patient rather than once per building. This is materially better recurrence than a one-off hospital construction supplier.

The company has 12 manufacturing facilities across four countries according to CRISIL's June 2026 rationale and more than 390 registered patents. Infusion therapy is still the largest category but its share fell to about 53% of sales in FY26 from 69% in FY23, indicating real diversification.

## 2. Why the magazine thesis is valid

Business Today highlighted India's medical-device market expanding from about $16.97bn in 2025 to $26.66bn by 2030, while import dependence remains high. Poly Medicure stood out because the magazine cited approximately ₹1,280 crore of FY26 international business, about 68% of revenue, exports to 100+ countries and a 50+ product development pipeline.

This creates four structural drivers:

1. Indian hospital/procedure growth.
2. Import substitution in higher-value devices.
3. Export expansion.
4. Product-mix migration from lower-value infusion products toward cardiology, orthopaedics, oncology and renal care.

The thesis therefore does not depend on one hospital chain or one government programme.

## 3. Financial quality

### FY26 consolidated

- Revenue: ~₹1,875 crore, +~12% YoY.
- Operating profit: ~₹442 crore.
- Operating margin: ~24% on statutory consolidated data; CRISIL's analytical group margin was ~26%.
- PAT: ~₹321 crore on statutory consolidated data.
- Operating cash flow: ~₹246 crore.
- Free cash flow was negative after a heavy investment/capex year.
- ROCE: ~14%.

Longer-term revenue and earnings growth has been strong, but FY26 was an important pause: consolidated revenue grew while operating profit and PAT did not keep pace.

### Core vs consolidated issue

The standalone business remains materially stronger than consolidated results. The latest Q1 FY27 numbers available in August 2026 showed a strong core business but weaker consolidated profit conversion as acquired overseas entities and new investments were absorbed.

This is the single most important current Phase-3 issue: **the acquisitions need to move from revenue addition to profit accretion.**

---

## 4. PendraCare and Citieffe acquisitions

### PendraCare

Poly Medicure agreed to acquire 90% of Netherlands-based PendraCare at an enterprise value of about ₹188.5 crore / €18.3m. The remaining 10% is linked to CY29 EBITDA and is due to be acquired in 2030.

PendraCare gives PolyMed technology and distribution in diagnostic/guiding cardiology catheters. Management has indicated that localisation of selected catheter production in India could reduce costs by roughly 25–30%, but also cautioned that market-share building takes time.

**Phase-3 interpretation:** strategically logical. The real test is whether localisation improves PendraCare's margins and whether PolyMed converts its Indian/global distribution into meaningful cardiology share.

### Citieffe

PolyMed acquired 100% of Citieffe Group at an enterprise value of about ₹324 crore / €31m. Citieffe generated CY24 revenue of ~€17.3m and EBITDA of ~€3.1m and specialises in orthopaedic trauma/extremities, with distribution across 25+ countries.

The deal expands PolyMed beyond disposable consumables into more differentiated orthopaedic devices. The purchase multiple was roughly 10x CY24 EBITDA.

**Phase-3 interpretation:** the strategic fit is good, but the market should not award PolyMed a higher multiple merely for buying growth. Citieffe must improve margins, cross-sell through PolyMed and produce ROIC above the company's cost of capital.

## 5. Moat / competitive position

### Strengths

- High-regulation medical devices create qualification and switching friction.
- Exports to demanding markets demonstrate manufacturing/quality capability.
- 390+ registered patents.
- Large, broad product catalogue and multiple therapy verticals.
- Labour-cost advantage versus global competitors.
- In-house tooling/R&D and significant manufacturing depth.
- Global distribution footprint.
- Recurring consumable nature of much of the portfolio.

### Limitations

CRISIL explicitly flags competition from global majors including Baxter, Becton Dickinson, B. Braun and Boston Scientific. It also notes PolyMed's R&D spend is lower than global peers, which matters increasingly as the company moves into higher-technology categories.

So the moat is strongest in **cost-efficient, regulated manufacturing + distribution + product breadth**, not proprietary blockbuster IP comparable with the world's largest medtech companies.

---

## 6. Cash flow and working capital — the main financial weakness

This is where the stock deserves more scrutiny.

FY26 operating cash flow was positive, but free cash flow was negative after significant capex/investment. Consolidated debtor days rose to roughly 103 days and inventory days/working-capital cycle also increased materially. Working-capital days were around 249 and the cash-conversion cycle was substantially longer than earlier years.

This is not a balance-sheet crisis: CRISIL describes liquidity as robust, net debt as negligible, cash/liquid investments around ₹700–800 crore and gearing below 0.10x, with planned annual capex largely funded internally.

But from an equity-investor perspective, **ROCE and cash conversion need to improve** before paying a premium multiple.

### What I want

- CFO/PAT sustainably above ~0.8x.
- debtor and inventory days stabilising/falling.
- free cash flow turning positive after the current capex/acquisition phase.
- ROCE moving back toward high teens rather than remaining around 12–15%.

---

## 7. Balance sheet

This is a major positive.

CRISIL upgraded the outlook to Positive while reaffirming AA-/A1+ in June 2026. It cited tangible net worth around ₹3,106 crore, negligible net debt, robust liquidity, cash/liquid investments around ₹700–800 crore, and expected annual cash accrual far above scheduled term-debt obligations.

Thus the acquisitions are an execution/capital-allocation risk, not presently a solvency risk.

## 8. Ownership / governance monitor

- Promoter holding: ~62.42% at Jun 2026.
- Promoter pledge: 0%.
- FII holding declined materially through FY26/Jun26.
- Domestic institutional/mutual-fund ownership increased.

Institutional flow alone is not a thesis signal.

Related-party transactions with promoter-linked/group entities should continue to be reviewed in annual/exchange filings, but this review found no evidence sufficient to label them a governance failure.

## 9. FY27 growth framework

Management's FY27 call summary indicates consolidated revenue guidance of roughly ₹2,300–2,400 crore and consolidated EBITDA margin around 23–25%, with standalone revenue around ₹1,900–1,950 crore and standalone EBITDA margin around 25–27%.

CRISIL independently expects roughly 20–22% FY27 revenue growth and medium-term profitability around 23–25%, providing useful external support for the broad growth framework.

The quality of FY27 growth matters more than the headline revenue number. We need:

- higher-tech categories gaining mix,
- acquisition margins improving,
- cash conversion improving,
- ROCE recovering.

## 10. Valuation — where the thesis becomes uncomfortable

At a latest reliable August 2026 reference price around ₹1,770, market capitalisation was roughly ₹18,000 crore and trailing consolidated P/E was approximately 50–56x.

That is not a cheap entry for a company with FY26 consolidated PAT down modestly, ROCE around 14% and elongated working capital.

### FY27 scenario analysis — not management guidance

Approximate share count: ~10.14 crore shares.

| FY27 PAT scenario | EPS | P/E at ₹1,770 | Price at 40x EPS |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ₹350cr | ~₹34.5 | ~51x | ~₹1,380 |
| ₹380cr | ~₹37.5 | ~47x | ~₹1,500 |
| ₹410cr | ~₹40.5 | ~44x | ~₹1,620 |

So even a strong FY27 does not make the stock obviously cheap at ~₹1,770.

### Fundamental interpretation

- Above ~₹1,800: valuation gives little room for execution disappointment; no chase.
- Roughly ₹1,600–1,650: first serious revalidation zone if earnings remain on track.
- Roughly ₹1,450–1,550: materially more attractive if consolidated margins/integration are improving.
- Roughly ₹1,300–1,400: high-interest valuation zone, provided there is no thesis deterioration.

These are valuation/research zones, not guaranteed technical supports or price targets.

## 11. Technical context

The verified historical chart shows a May/June 2026 correction toward the ₹1,300–1,400 area followed by a recovery and base around ₹1,600–1,700 in July. The 52-week range around the latest snapshots was approximately ₹1,182–₹2,145/2,148.

The stock therefore is neither near the lows nor at the old high. Given the valuation, the preferred setup is a correction/retest rather than chasing a momentum breakout.

## 12. Bull / Base / Bear

### Bull

- Consolidated revenue grows >20%.
- Standalone margin remains ~26–28%.
- PendraCare/Citieffe margins improve through localisation/cross-selling.
- Cardiology/orthopaedics/renal/oncology become >50% of mix over time.
- Working capital normalises and FCF turns positive.
- ROCE returns toward ~18%+.

Then PolyMed can justify a premium medtech multiple and potentially compound earnings at high teens/20%+.

### Base

- Revenue compounds 15–18%.
- Acquisitions add revenue but only slowly improve margins.
- Consolidated margin stays 23–25%.
- ROCE remains 13–15% for longer.

The business compounds, but buying at 50x+ earnings limits shareholder returns through valuation de-rating.

### Bear

- International/acquired businesses remain structurally lower-margin.
- Product diversification is slower than expected.
- Inventory/receivables keep rising.
- FCF remains weak despite PAT growth.
- Consolidated operating margin falls below 20% for a sustained period.
- Another large acquisition absorbs the remaining balance-sheet liquidity before existing integrations prove themselves.

In that scenario the market can compress the multiple sharply even if revenue keeps growing.

## 15. Final decision framework

### Business quality
Strong.

### Revenue recurrence
Very strong relative to hospital/project businesses.

### Balance sheet
Strong.

### Growth runway
Strong: India localisation, exports and higher-value therapies.

### Execution today
Mixed: core business strong; consolidated integration still needs proof.

### Cash conversion
Needs improvement.

### Valuation
Expensive enough that execution must be very good.

## Phase-3 verdict

**Poly Medicure is a company worth owning at the right valuation, but the latest ~₹1,770 reference level does not provide enough margin of safety to chase.**

The cleanest entry trigger is not merely a lower price. It is:

> **Price correction + consolidated margin stabilisation + acquisition profitability + improving working capital.**

If the stock reaches roughly ₹1,450–1,600 while the business remains on track, re-run valuation immediately. If earnings accelerate enough that forward valuation falls toward ~40x without the stock correcting, that can also create an entry.

If the next results show strong revenue but no improvement in consolidated PAT/cash flow, remain patient.

### Single disprove metric

**If consolidated operating margin falls below ~20% on a sustained basis while working capital continues deteriorating, the premium-compounder thesis is weakened materially.**
